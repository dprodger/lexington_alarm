Summary Notes from Lexington Alarm Meeting Sept 16 2025

MEETING NARRATIVE

This meeting of the Lexington Alarm group focused primarily on two major initiatives: organizing a rally for October 18th and developing a sustained campaign against ICE operations in Burlington and at Hanscom Field.

THE OCTOBER 18TH RALLY DISCUSSION

The meeting opened with discussion of a planned rally themed around "No King, No Tyranny" - connecting to national protests against authoritarian overreach. The most substantive debate centered on the format and speakers for the event.

Initially, there was consideration of inviting Bill McKibben as a speaker, but Sarah quickly raised concerns that the rally shouldn't have "just an environmental viewpoint." This sparked a broader discussion about whether to have traditional keynote speakers or something more grassroots.

A significant portion of time was spent debating between an "open mic" format versus pre-organized speakers. Several members advocated for having ordinary people share personal testimonies about how they've been affected - from lost NIH grants to workplace DEI protections being stripped. However, Avram raised a critical concern that drew agreement: an open mic could allow opponents to speak and create confrontational situations that "plays right into their hands."

The group ultimately coalesced around a hybrid approach - having pre-selected ordinary people with powerful personal stories, but organized and rehearsed beforehand. Jonina emphasized the critical importance of sound quality, noting they would need a professional sound system for 1,000 people (costing $1,200-1,500) and proper training on microphone use.

Oct 18th Decisions:

1. Form October 18th Rally Committee - Approved with sufficient volunteers. Will organize speakers, sound system, and logistics. Theme: "No Kings, No Tyranny"

2. Secure Sound System and Stage Risers - Budgeted $1,200-$1,500 for professional equipment

THE ICE CAMPAIGN DISCUSSION

The ICE discussion consumed the largest portion of the meeting and revealed the complexity of organizing sustained resistance. The conversation began with acknowledging they need a "very large numerical group of people who are committed to doing whatever is necessary to slow down or affect ICE operations."

A major revelation came about deportation flights from Hanscom Field. Many attendees, including long-time activists, were surprised to learn that ICE had resumed using Hanscom after previously stopping. This knowledge gap highlighted the need for better information gathering and sharing.

The discussion revealed multiple simultaneous efforts already underway: Wednesday standouts at the ICE facility drawing 400+ people, newer Thursday standouts organized by Indivisible groups, small group protests organized via Discord throughout the week, and a need for "verifiers" - trained volunteers who respond to ICE sighting reports from LUCE.

Charlie suggested separating the Hanscom airport issues from the Burlington ICE facility work, but the group recognized these were interconnected, as ICE uses Hanscom for deportation flights.

A significant challenge emerged around coordination and communication. Kate from Mass 50501 noted that "different groups use different communication methods" - younger activists use Discord, older groups prefer email, and some use Signal. This fragmentation was hindering effective mobilization.

KEY TENSIONS AND RESOLUTIONS

The meeting revealed several tensions:

1. Scope creep - Multiple speakers worried about taking on too much at once

2. Communication silos - Different groups weren't effectively sharing information

3. Tactical questions - How confrontational to be, whether to focus on awareness-raising first

4. Capacity concerns - Whether they had enough volunteers to sustain multiple efforts

The group resolved these by agreeing to form dedicated committees for both the October 18th rally and ongoing ICE work, recognizing that research and coordination with existing efforts should be the first priority, and acknowledging that different volunteers would gravitate toward different aspects of the work.

ICE Decisions:

Create ICE Task Force/Working Group - Approved with 6-8 initial volunteers. Will coordinate with existing groups to see what is most needed - bearing witness, LUCE, Heatwave, MA 50501, Indivisible etc.


LETTER DISCUSSION SUMMARY


The group discussed a letter to the Lexington Select Board regarding the lowering of flags for Charlie Kirk. The letter asks the Select Board to establish a policy for flag-lowering decisions rather than automatically following presidential proclamations that may subject the flag to partisan considerations. 

Key Discussion Points:

Authorship: Karin wrote the initial draft and will incorporate community values into the final version

Format: Decision to have one unified letter (not two separate ones) under Lexington Alarm's name

Signatures: Debate over collecting physical vs. online signatures (decision: both methods will be used)
Timing: Whether to present before or after their permit application on Sept 29th
Publication: Consideration of publishing in the Lexington Observer
Follow-up: Questions about whether this is symbolic or aimed at actual policy change

Decision:

The group decided to:

Proceed with one letter incorporating community values
Collect both physical and online signatures
Not rush the submission - gather signatures first, then submit after their January 29th permit meeting
Focus remains on making a political statement rather than expecting immediate policy change
Consider potential town meeting petition as future action if needed

The letter aims to ask the Select Board to "consider a modest proposal" regarding flag-lowering policies, emphasizing local control over such decisions rather than automatic compliance with federal proclamations.
